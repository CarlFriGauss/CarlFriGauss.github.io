The model behind the picture

This is a zero-mean Gaussian process with a squared-exponential (RBF) kernel:

k(x, x′) = σf² exp(−(x − x′)² / (2ℓ²))

Here ℓ is the length scale and σf is the signal standard deviation. Observations follow y = f(x) + ε, with independent Gaussian noise of standard deviation σn. The training covariance adds σn² to its diagonal. Parameters are chosen by you rather than fitted automatically.

The thin curves are joint samples of the latent function, initially from the prior and then from the posterior as you add observations. The underlying random draws stay fixed while you add points, so you can see conditioning change the functions. “Draw new functions” generates a fresh set without changing your data. With positive observation noise, posterior functions need not pass exactly through the observations.

Repeated inputs at zero noise may need a small numerical regularization, which is reported below the plot. For conflicting observations at the same x, a positive noise level is a more appropriate model.

The browser version is self-contained and accepts points directly. The original Python application source remains available separately.
